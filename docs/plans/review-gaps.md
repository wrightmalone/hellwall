# What They Are Billions players praise and dislike, against Hellwall

For going through together. The sources are Steam (80% positive of about 19k English reviews, 83% recently), the top positive and negative Steam reviews and forum threads, Game Informer, GameSpot and IGN via OpenCritic, TheSixthAxis, and TAB's own patch log. Reddit couldn't be reached, so player voices come from Steam. Each item ends with my recommendation.

## Praised, and we're missing it or only partly have it

1. **The spectacle of a huge horde.** It's among the most-mentioned pleasures: thousands on screen and the final wave pouring in. Our engine handles 20,000 (the bench), and the Convergence is now 14,500, so the numbers are there. What's missing is the moment itself: sound, weight, the screen reacting.
   *Recommend:* a spectacle pass on the Convergence. A swelling roar as it lands, the ground darkening under the mass, some light camera shake at the walls, and heavier death marks. That's cheap, and it's what people remember and share.

2. **Sharing maps (the Workshop).** Their editor and Workshop was TAB's main late investment. We have the editor and a weekly challenge, but hand-made maps can't leave your machine.
   *Recommend:* export/import a map as a file now. The Steam Workshop comes when we're on Steam.

3. **Brutal difficulty as the point.** Fans love learning by failing, and TAB kept adding levels (Nightmare, then Apocalypse, and raised Brutal by 20–50% after veterans complained). Our bot wins 17 of 24 on Normal, which may be soft for veterans. We have Easy to Nightmare, but no "harder than Nightmare" and no custom percentage.
   *Recommend:* a custom difficulty slider in skirmish (waves %, horde speed %, starting resources), plus one tier above Nightmare once you've played Nightmare yourself.

4. **An ironman option.** A minority defends no-saves as the whole point.
   *Recommend:* an optional "Ironman" toggle (one save that's kept on exit, deleted on loss). It's cheap, and it gives the hardcore crowd their game without forcing it on anyone.

5. **Two small conveniences TAB added after launch.** Shift-queued waypoints (shift-right-click to chain moves) and keyboard zoom. We have neither.
   *Recommend:* both. Shift-queue matters most for patrols round a base.

6. **Its art and music.** Critics single them out. Ours are placeholder CC0 packs and synthesized music.
   *Recommend:* not now, but it's the biggest commercial gap, and it wants a budget line once the game's loop is settled.

## Disliked in TAB, and whether we copied it

1. **No mid-mission saves.** By far the top complaint. **We avoided it** (save anywhere). Keep it; the ironman toggle above covers the other side.

2. **One stray unit ends a run**: one breach cascades through the base, often from something the player didn't control (a soldier's shot waking a pack, a unit wandering). **We copied the cascade on purpose.** Saves soften it, the noise view (F4) exists, and packs now go for the soldiers who woke them, not the base. Still worth deciding:
   *Recommend:* make the first breach impossible to miss. A distinct horn, a red edge on the screen, and the alert jumping to it the first time a building is possessed. A second or two of warning is the difference between "unfair" and "I should have watched that".

3. **A weak campaign**: repetitive "same map, harder" missions, spawns you only learn by dying, and unfair final missions. This is the second biggest complaint, and critics say survival was playtested but the campaign wasn't. **Partly our risk.** Our nine missions are generated maps with objectives, and several are survive-until-day-N.
   *Recommend:* a campaign pass before any story work.
   - Every mission shows its threats up front: the briefing names the wave sides and the gates.
   - No hidden spawns.
   - At least three missions with a shape of their own: the bridge map, a river crossing, an island.
   - Every mission playtested by you, not only by the bot (the bot can't close gates right now anyway).

4. **The final wave piles onto one or two walls while the rest of the defence idles**, and TAB didn't say where. **We copied the pile-up on purpose** (60% on one side), but it's announced 10 minutes ahead and marked on the map, which removes the "gotcha". This matches what you described with the bridge. Keep it, and watch whether it feels fair in play.

5. **Slow pacing and no fast-forward.** **We avoided it** (Tab cycles the speed).

6. **No respec, and a bad early tech pick can soft-lock a campaign.** **We avoided it**: research and patrons last one run, not the whole campaign. Nothing needed.

7. **Top difficulties are luck-dependent** (a bad map means a lost run). **Partly copied.** Map generation guarantees minimum rock, forest and ore, but not a good start.
   *Recommend:* a "new map" reroll in skirmish before the first minute, and a check in map generation that the Keep has buildable ground and wood nearby.

8. **Thin variety** (few map themes, units that blur together). **Partly.** We have 4–5 map kinds and 7 soldier types.
   *Recommend:* covered by the map-shape work in 3.

9. **The developers moved on** (no content after 1.1, and prickly forum moderation). Not about the game, but it's what a commercial release is judged on after launch.
   *Recommend:* plan a post-launch cadence (maps, weekly challenges) before we ship.

## What TAB's own patches tell us mattered

- **Within a week of launch:** campaign backups, an easier default difficulty, and early missions without time limits. They listened on saves and difficulty, but only halfway.
- **More difficulty levels at both ends.** Both audiences mattered.
- **Quality of life:** select-all-of-type (we have double-click and the card's top row), shift waypoints and keyboard zoom (we have neither).
- **Their biggest late investment was the editor and Workshop.**

## My suggested order

1. Make the first breach impossible to miss (small, and it answers the most-felt unfairness).
2. Shift-queued waypoints and keyboard zoom (small).
3. A campaign pass: threats shown in briefings, no hidden spawns, the bridge map and two other shaped maps, a playtest of each.
4. The Convergence spectacle pass.
5. Ironman toggle, custom difficulty slider, and a map reroll in skirmish.
6. Map export/import (the Workshop later).
7. Art and music, when there's a budget.
