Production brief · art, sound, music

Hellwall: what it needs to look and sound like a finished game

A list of every visual and sound the game uses or will use, what stands in for it today, and how much it matters. It's for artists, a sound designer, and a composer, so the rest can be put off until they've read it.

GenreColony-building survival against a horde (They Are Billions, in a dark medieval world)
ViewIsometric, 2:1, camera 30° down and 45° round
ScaleUp to 20,000 demons on the map; a town of a few hundred buildings
EngineGodot 4, 2D sprites baked from 3D models

The game, and what stands in now

You found a colony on holy ground, build it up through sixty days, wall it, and hold it against waves of demons that grow until the last night, the Convergence, when everything comes at once. A breach cascades: one possessed house turns the next. The tone is grim and weighty, not heroic; the demons are many and small, the town is fragile and warm.

What's in the game now is placeholder, all free to use:

  • Buildings: KayKit's Medieval Hexagon pack, rendered from our camera. Walls are KayKit's castle wall and a timber palisade made from simple shapes.
  • Soldiers and workers: Quaternius's RPG Characters, tinted per role.
  • Demons: Quaternius's Ultimate Monsters.
  • Trees, rocks, grass: Quaternius's Stylized Nature pack.
  • Projectiles and effects: lines and circles drawn in code.
  • Every sound and the music: synthesized in code at start-up. None of it is recorded.

How art gets into the game

Everything on the map is a 2D sprite rendered from a 3D model by our own baking tools, so it all shares one camera and light. The easiest thing to deliver is a 3D model (glTF or FBX), which we bake; hand-drawn 2D sprites work too, to these specs.

  • Figures (soldiers, workers, demons): 8 facings, 6 frames per animation, 72 px square cells, feet at a fixed point. Animations we use: walk, attack, and for some a work loop (chop, dig, sow, reap). Death animations are welcome.
  • Buildings: drawn at 132 px across one tile's diamond, shown at half size; footprints of 1×1, 2×2 or 3×3 tiles. Each needs a construction state and ideally a damaged one.
  • Walls: joining pieces, a post plus an arm toward each neighbour, so a line of them reads as one wall with corners.
  • Readability first: at normal zoom a soldier is about 30 px tall and a demon smaller. Silhouettes and colour must tell kinds apart at that size, among hundreds.

Sound effects

Thirteen sounds are synthesized now: a shot, a boom, a horn, a howl, a burst, built, possessed, fallen, promoted, felled, a voice blip, victory and the Convergence roar. All of it wants replacing. Three things matter more than usual here:

Scale. With thousands of demons on screen, sounds can't be one per demon. Each wants 3 to 5 variations, and the horde itself wants ambient beds at three sizes (a handful, a pack, the Convergence) that the game fades between by how many are in view.

  • Format: WAV (48 kHz, 24-bit) masters; the game uses OGG. Short effects mono, as they're placed in the world; ambience and stingers stereo.
  • Length: combat sounds short (under a second); events up to five seconds.
  • Loudness: a consistent level across the set, with the alarm and the Convergence the loudest things in the game.

Music

The direction: moodier and slower than Red Alert 2 or Tiberian Sun, less driving. Nearer the weight of Frostpunk or They Are Billions: a town holding out in the cold and dark, sorrow as much as threat, the horde as weather more than enemy.

The game already plays music in layers. A calm bed plays under everything, and a battle layer on the same chords fades in with how many demons are in sight. The placeholder does this with a pad and a drum. The real score should keep the idea, so the music rises with the fight instead of switching tracks.

Stems matter most. For the in-game music, the ideal delivery is each track as stems (a bed, a tension layer, a battle layer) at one tempo and key, cut to loop, so the game can mix them live. Finished stereo tracks work too, as a fallback.

Licensing: we'd need rights to use the music in a commercial game on Steam and other stores, in trailers and store videos, for the life of the game. A non-exclusive licence is fine. A soundtrack release (Steam's soundtrack DLC) would be a separate, welcome arrangement. The stems are needed for the game itself, not for publication.

What to do first

  1. Projectiles and impacts: arrows, bolts, the Bombard, bile, bursts, hits on walls. These are the most visible placeholder, and every fight is made of them.
  2. Sound for combat and the horde: bows, impacts, demon deaths, the horde beds, and the alarm and horn. A silent or synthetic fight undersells everything else.
  3. Music: the town tracks with their battle stems, then the menu theme and the Convergence.
  4. The demons, the Imp first (it's most of what's on screen), then Hound, Thrall and Gargoyle.
  5. The talking heads and the title, which the campaign and the Steam page need.
  6. The town: the Keep, homes, gatherers, towers. The stand-ins are passable here, so this can come after the fight looks and sounds right.
